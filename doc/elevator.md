# contacc

Most of what we share online is shaped by the question "who will see this?" — and that invisible pressure changes what we say, how we say it, and what we leave out entirely. Even our "private" posts are really just public posts with a smaller audience.

contacc starts from a different premise: your data should belong to you, live on hardware you control, and be shared only when and how you decide — not according to the terms of a platform that monetizes your attention.

At its simplest, contacc is a personal server for your photos, videos, and writing. You share things directly with specific people, who access them from your server. No intermediary, no algorithm deciding what they see, no copy of your content sitting on someone else's infrastructure.

It works in both directions. Your client connects to the servers of people you follow, pulling their feeds directly from their hardware. What you see is what they chose to share with you — not a platform's guess at what will keep you engaged. You can connect to as many people as you like; your client aggregates their feeds into a single view, filtered and presented however suits you. An AI can help here too — summarizing what's new across your contacts, surfacing things you'd actually care about, making connections you might have missed.

The more interesting idea is what you can do when the data is genuinely yours. You can keep a private journal — what we call an inner monologue — and know it will never be shared unless you choose to. You can give an AI access to your records selectively, for a specific purpose, and revoke that access when you're done. You can use AI to help you remember things, see patterns in your own thinking, or draft something to share — drawing on your private record without exposing it.

The goal is a personal data layer that works the way a thoughtful person would: sharing deliberately, receiving without distortion, holding back freely, and staying in control of what crosses the boundary.
